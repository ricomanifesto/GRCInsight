Executive Summary

Organizations face an accelerating cadence of actively exploited vulnerabilities across critical infrastructure components. In September 2026, threat actors moved rapidly from disclosure to exploitation for flaws in Check Point Security Gateway VPN ([1]), WordPress ([2]), MikroTik RouterOS ([3]), F5 BIG-IP APM ([5]), and a Chrome-Windows zero-day chain ([6]). The compression of time-to-exploit demands immediate patching workflows and compensating controls for assets that cannot be updated instantly.

Supply chain attack surfaces have expanded beyond traditional software dependencies. Malicious Terraform providers were discovered in the HashiCorp Registry ([9]), while GitLab's issue-email feature exposes privileged tokens that enable unauthorized code pushes and CI/CD execution ([8]; [10]). These vectors bypass conventional perimeter defenses and require revised vendor risk assessments and developer credential hygiene programs.

Endpoint detection and response (EDR) efficacy is being challenged by novel evasion techniques. A process parameter-poisoning method injects code into process initialization structures without invoking monitored Windows APIs ([7]). Simultaneously, financially motivated actors are leveraging open-source AI agent frameworks to automate skimming campaigns across hundreds of retailers, stealing over 600,000 credit card records ([11]). These developments signal a shift toward AI-augmented offensive operations that outpace signature-based defenses.

Geographic threat concentration warrants attention for organizations with Gulf region exposure. The United Arab Emirates and Saudi Arabia together absorbed 50% of all cyberattacks recorded across the Gulf in the first half of 2026 ([12]). This regional intensity amplifies risk for sectors including energy, finance, and logistics operating in or serving those markets.
