## Executive Summary

Critical infrastructure and identity management systems face unprecedented exploitation pressure this quarter, with multiple maximum-severity vulnerabilities actively targeted in production environments. CISA has added three Linux kernel flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, including CVE-2025-39682 rated CVSS 9.8, signaling immediate patching obligations for federal agencies and critical infrastructure operators [CISA Flags Three Linux Kernel Vulnerabilities Exploited in the Wild](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/cisa-flags-three-linux-kernel.html). Simultaneously, authentication bypass flaws in Cisco Identity Services Engine (CVE-2026-76460, CVSS 10.0) and Microsoft Azure AI Foundry (CVE-2026-85889, CVSS 10.0) demonstrate that identity and access management platforms remain primary attack vectors [Cisco Zero-Day Highlights API Endpoint Authentication Issues](https://www.darkreading.com/vulnerabilities-threats/cisco-zero-day-api-endpoint-authentication-issues), [Microsoft Patches CVSS 10.0 Azure AI Foundry Flaw Enabling Unauthorized Privilege Escalation](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/microsoft-patches-cvss-100-azure-ai.html).

AI-enabled attack chains are compressing the window between vulnerability disclosure and reliable exploitation, as demonstrated by researchers using Claude Opus 5 to chain flaws and compromise OpenAI staff accounts and internal repositories [Claude Opus 5 Helped Researchers Take Over OpenAI Staff Accounts via Chained Flaws](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/claude-opus-5-helped-researchers-take.html). The BragJack proof-of-concept further illustrates how malicious browser extensions can hijack AI assistants across Chrome, Edge, Opera Neon, Perplexity Comet, and Claude, earning over $20,000 in bounties and two CVE assignments [BragJack attacks hijack AI browser agents through malicious extensions](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/bragjack-attacks-hijack-ai-browser-agents-through-malicious-extensions/). These developments indicate that traditional quarterly vulnerability management cycles are no longer aligned with adversary speed.

State-sponsored campaigns continue to scale with measurable financial impact. The North Korean WaterPlum group compromised at least 30,000 devices globally from December 2025 through July 2026, exfiltrating more than $10.7 million in cryptocurrency [North Korean WaterPlum hackers infected 30,000 devices worldwide](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/north-korean-waterplum-hackers-infected-30-000-devices-worldwide/). Meanwhile, extortion groups are targeting each other—ShinyHunters breached the Clop ransomware operation's leak site, stealing server data and onion service private keys [ShinyHunters hacks Clop leak site, threatens to extort ransomware gang](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/shinyhunters-hacks-clop-leak-site-threatens-to-extort-ransomware-gang/). This intra-threat-actor conflict creates unpredictable collateral risk for organizations caught in crossfire.

Identity visibility has emerged as the foundational control for modern security programs, with stolen and misused credentials remaining the most frequently reported initial access vector per Verizon DBIR [Identity Visibility in 2026: The Foundation of Identity Security](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/identity-visibility-in-2026-foundation.html). Organizations must prioritize continuous identity posture assessment, AI-assisted threat modeling, and exploitability validation over severity scoring alone [Can You Prove a New CVE Is Exploitable Before Attackers Do?
