Executive Summary

Active exploitation of critical infrastructure vulnerabilities has accelerated across enterprise software supply chains, with CISA adding five actively exploited flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog affecting JFrog Artifactory, ConnectWise ScreenConnect, and MikroTik RouterOS [1]. Simultaneously, the Dutch NCSC has warned of imminent exploitation of two critical Check Point VPN vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-85102, CVE-2026-85103) [2], while GitLab disclosed a maximum-severity file-read flaw (CVE-2026-85706) attracting in-the-wild probes within hours of disclosure [3]. Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center authentication bypass (CVE-2026-20079) has been leveraged by three distinct threat clusters for credential theft and Qilin ransomware deployment [4].

Artificial intelligence has emerged as a dual-use force multiplier: threat actors are weaponizing generative AI to produce one million personalized fraud emails in three days [8], abusing Claude to extract secrets from 1.8 million Android applications [7], and deploying OpenAI agent swarms to achieve remote code execution on RubyDoc servers via the RubyGems supply chain [6]. Enterprise SOCs face a new alert class driven by legitimate AI tool adoption across developer and non-technical workforces [5], while passkey-themed social engineering campaigns target Microsoft 365 credentials [12].

Regulatory momentum is shifting toward mandatory transparency: CISA has called for more guidance and less spin as cyber outages escalate, signaling a push for transparent breach notification and incident response protocols [10]. The Florida DMV breach via stolen police credentials [9] underscores third-party access governance gaps across public-sector identity ecosystems.

  3. Audit third-party credential access for government and critical infrastructure partners; revoke standing police/agency credentials from Florida DMV-style databases and enforce just-in-time privileged access.
  4. Activate AI-abuse monitoring for anomalous API call volumes to generative AI platforms (Claude, OpenAI) from corporate IP ranges; correlate with data exfiltration signals.

Near-Term (30–90 Days)

  1. Implement AI governance framework covering approved models, data classification for prompts, agent permissions, and SOC alert tuning for AI-tool-generated noise [5].
  2. Harden identity providers against passkey-themed phishing with phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2/WebAuthn), conditional access policies, and user education on single sign-on social engineering [12].
  3. Align breach notification playbooks with CISA transparency expectations: predefined communication templates, stakeholder mapping, and regulatory deadline tracking [10].
  4. Establish software supply chain integrity controls for package managers (RubyGems, npm, PyPI) including signed commits, provenance verification, and automated dependency scanning.

Strategic (90+ Days)

  1. Integrate AI threat modeling into enterprise risk assessments addressing model abuse, agentic autonomy, and synthetic content generation at scale [8]; [11].
  2. Adopt zero-trust network segmentation for management planes (FMC, VPN concentrators, router management interfaces) to limit blast radius of authentication bypass exploits.
  3. Formalize regulatory horizon scanning for GDPR, CCPA, PCI-DSS, and NIST CSF alignment as breach notification requirements converge across jurisdictions.
