Executive Summary

Critical vulnerability disclosures in September 2026 reveal a cluster of maximum-severity flaws affecting widely deployed enterprise infrastructure, including Cisco Identity Services Engine (CVE-2026-76460, CVSS 10.0) [4] and Microsoft Azure AI Foundry (CVE-2026-85889, CVSS 10.0) [5]. Both vulnerabilities enable unauthenticated privilege escalation or authentication bypass, demanding immediate patch validation across identity and AI workloads.

Active exploitation campaigns are accelerating across multiple vectors. CISA added three Linux kernel vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, including CVE-2025-39682 (CVSS 9.8) [3], while Fortinet confirmed in-the-wild exploitation of Orkes Conductor CVE-2026-58138 (CVSS 9.8/9.3) [2]. The North Korean WaterPlum campaign compromised at least 30,000 devices globally from December 2025 through July 2026, exfiltrating over $10.7 million in cryptocurrency [8].

AI-enabled attack chains are emerging as a force multiplier for threat actors. Researchers demonstrated that Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 could chain two flaws to compromise OpenAI employee accounts and access internal code repositories [6]. Separately, the BragJack proof-of-concept hijacks AI browser agents across Chrome, Edge, Opera Neon, Perplexity Comet, and Claude via a single malicious extension using a "Prompt Forcing" technique [7].

Identity compromise remains the dominant initial access vector, reinforcing the urgency of identity visibility programs [11]. The ShinyHunters breach of the Clop ransomware leak site illustrates escalating conflict among extortion actors [9]. Meanwhile, consumer-facing AI services such as the "Talking Tilly" video call platform perform biometric face scanning and mood sensing, raising data protection questions under CCPA and GDPR [12].
