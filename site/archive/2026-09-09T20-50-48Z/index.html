Executive Summary

Three maximum-severity vulnerabilities are under active exploitation and demand immediate remediation: a Chrome V8 zero-day enabling sandbox escape [1], an unauthenticated SAP kernel flaw rated CVSS 10.0 [3], and an N-able N-central pre-authentication RCE added to the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a September 11, 2026 federal action deadline [4]. Risk managers should treat these as priority-one patching events and verify compensating controls where immediate updates are not feasible.

Identity and access control assumptions are being invalidated by AI-era attack techniques. Researchers demonstrate workflow identity hijacking that bypasses standard controls through unauthenticated entry points [6], while infostealer malware harvests replayable AI service tokens that circumvent multi-factor authentication [7]. Account recovery flows have become the preferred vector for MFA bypass, turning service-desk verification into a critical control gap [8].

Third-party and supply chain risk materialized in a healthcare data breach at Veradigm traced to a vendor incident [5], while the N-able RCE directly threatens managed-service-provider infrastructure used across sectors [4]. Over 36,000 internet-exposed Plex media servers remain unpatched against disclosed flaws [12], illustrating persistent asset-management failures that expand organizational attack surface.

Operational readiness for vulnerability response is a strategic differentiator. The industry is grappling with the "Are we exposed?" question across scanners, endpoints, cloud inventories, SBOMs, and repositories [9], while a Microsoft Defender patch bypass (ShieldCrash) for CVE-2026-69414 demonstrates that vendor fixes cannot be assumed effective without validation [2].

Recommendations for Action

Immediate (0–72 hours)

  • Deploy Chrome updates addressing CVE-2026-87491 across all endpoints [1]
  • Apply SAP security notes for CVE-2026-44756; restrict network access to SAP Extended Passport Processing where patching is delayed [3]
  • Patch N-able N-central instances to meet CISA KEV deadline of September 11, 2026; validate MSP partners have remediated [4]
  • Verify Microsoft Defender detections for CVE-2026-69414/ShieldCrash; deploy additional endpoint telemetry [2]

Short-Term (1–4 weeks)

  • Implement phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2/WebAuthn) and harden account-recovery workflows with identity-proofing at service desks [8]
  • Rotate all AI service API keys and tokens; enforce short-lived, scoped credentials; monitor for anomalous AI platform access [7]
  • Deploy zero-trust network segmentation for AI/ML workloads; inspect unauthenticated entry points for workflow identity hijacking [6]
  • Conduct third-party risk reassessment for vendors with access to sensitive data or critical infrastructure; require evidence of N-able and SAP patching [5], [4]

Medium-Term (1–3 months)

  • Establish continuous exposure-mapping capability integrating vulnerability scanners, cloud inventories, SBOMs, and endpoint data to answer "Are we exposed?" within hours [9]
  • Implement runtime sandbox enforcement and attestation for AI coding agents; prohibit self-modifying sandbox configurations [10]
  • Execute internet-facing asset discovery; remediate or isolate unpatched services (e.g., Plex, Alby Hub) [12], [11]
  • Formalize patch-validation process: test vendor fixes in staging, monitor for bypass PoCs, maintain compensating controls until efficacy confirmed
