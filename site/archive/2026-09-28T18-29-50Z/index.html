Executive Summary

Active exploitation of critical infrastructure vulnerabilities demands immediate patching and compensating controls. CISA has added two Citrix NetScaler remote code execution flaws (CVE-2026-88771, CVE-2026-88772) and a Microsoft SharePoint code injection vulnerability (CVE-2026-65660) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, confirming active exploitation in the wild [1] [2] [4]. Citrix has released security updates for both NetScaler vulnerabilities [2].

Threat actors are bypassing traditional perimeter defenses and targeting identity and AI infrastructure. The ShinyHunters extortion group is using a URL-encoding technique to circumvent web application firewall protections for Oracle PeopleSoft CVE-2026-35273, enabling continued exploitation [3]. Simultaneously, infostealer malware has compromised AI account credentials across more than 80,000 corporate domains, fueling an emerging LLMjacking threat vector [6]. A new botnet, Carbonato, is hijacking exposed Docker daemons to deploy Telegram-controlled Hermes AI agents [8].

Cloud identity compromise enables destructive operations at scale. The JADEPUFFER threat actor (tracked as Storm-3168 by Microsoft) leveraged compromised service principals to delete Azure resources over an 18-hour period in June 2026 [10]. This incident underscores the risk of excessive permissions on non-human identities and the need for rigorous service principal governance.

Financial crime and law enforcement actions signal escalating consequences for attackers. A $387.5 million cryptocurrency heist attributed to North Korean actors targeted the Bitget exchange [9], while Dutch authorities arrested a ShinyHunters associate, triggering retaliatory escalation including data theft from the FBI and extortion of the Cl0p ransomware group [5]. A former U.S. Army soldier received a 70-month sentence for hacking and extorting at least 10 technology and telecommunications firms between April 2023 and December 2024 [11].
