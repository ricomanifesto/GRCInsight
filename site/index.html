Key messages

  1. Critical vulnerabilities in Citrix NetScaler application delivery controllers and active exploitation of a patched flaw in ConnectWise ScreenConnect remote access software require immediate asset identification and patch verification across infrastructure and managed service provider dependencies. Authentication bypass and denial-of-service risks in NetScaler devices undermine access controls, while the ScreenConnect campaign extends compromise risk to downstream customers through trusted MSP channels. Vulnerability and asset owners should confirm affected versions are inventoried, patches applied, and MSP access scopes reviewed for excessive privileges. [1]; [2]

  2. Emerging threats include AI agents manipulating financial transactions without triggering alerts, state-level compromise of telecom infrastructure to intercept SMS multi-factor codes, and blockchain complexities that hobble security controls for exchange compromises. An international law enforcement takedown of malware infrastructure seized millions of stolen credentials, necessitating credential exposure checks. Identity and access owners should review AI agent permissions and implement transaction approvals; monitoring owners should evaluate SMS 2FA reliance and signaling telemetry; incident response leads should verify credential exposure from seized datasets; governance owners should reassess control assumptions for blockchain environments. [3]; [7]; [8]; [4]

High priorityVulnerability management

NetScaler ADC, Citrix NetScaler Gateway, exposes authentication bypass and DoS vulnerabilities

Authentication bypass in widely deployed application delivery controllers undermines access controls and could allow unauthorized administrative access. Vulnerability and asset owners should confirm affected versions are identified, patches applied, and compensating controls validated for any unpatched instances.

02

High priorityThird-party risk

ConnectWise ScreenConnect deployments, enables MSP attack paths via exploited flaw

Exploitation of a patched flaw in remote access software used by managed service providers extends risk to downstream customers. Supplier risk owners should verify that all ScreenConnect instances are updated and that MSP access scopes are reviewed for excessive privileges.

Accountable owner
Supplier risk owner
Evidence to request
Supplier access scope, control responsibilities and evidence of remediation assurance.
Decision trigger
If the supplier dependency exists and assurance is insufficient, request evidence or escalate the assurance gap to its owner.
Reference controls
ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.19 (supplier relationships), A.5.21 (ICT supply chain), A.5.22 (monitoring and review of supplier services); NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC (cybersecurity supply chain risk management)
03

Medium priorityAccess management

AI agent built to automate invoice processing, allows manipulation of vendor bank details

AI agents with financial transaction capabilities can bypass traditional email security controls, creating unauthorized payment changes. Identity and access owners should review AI agent permissions, implement transaction approval workflows, and monitor for anomalous agent behavior.

Accountable owner
Identity and access owner
Evidence to request
Access scope, authentication coverage, session revocation and a permission-test result.
Decision trigger
If a permission gap is reproduced, assign a control correction and verify it; otherwise retain the test evidence.
Reference controls
ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.15 (access control), A.5.18 (access rights), A.8.2 (privileged access rights); NIST CSF 2.0 PR.AA (identity management, authentication and access control)

High priorityMonitoring and detection

Asian mobile operators, compromised to intercept SMS 2FA codes and spy

Compromise of telecom infrastructure to intercept multi-factor authentication codes undermines identity verification for targeted users. Monitoring and detection owners should assess telemetry for anomalous signaling traffic and evaluate reliance on SMS-based 2FA for high-value accounts.

Accountable owner
Detection and monitoring owner

Decision trigger

Medium priorityIncident response

Takedown of malware infrastructure reduces active threat but stolen credentials remain in circulation. Incident response leads should verify if organizational credentials appear in seized datasets and initiate password resets for affected accounts.

Medium priorityGovernance and accountability

security controls designed to limit the damage, hobbled by blockchain complexities

Blockchain-specific attack mechanisms can bypass traditional security controls, limiting damage containment capabilities. Governance and accountability owners should review control assumptions for blockchain-based assets and ensure incident procedures address smart contract and sweeper risks.
