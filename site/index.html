Key messages

  1. CISA added an actively exploited Apple authentication flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog while SonicWall confirmed critical vulnerabilities in end-of-life management systems will remain unpatched, and an OpenSSH defect allows authentication bypass on password-disabled accounts. These developments expand the set of unpatchable or actively exploited assets requiring immediate inventory and compensating control verification. Vulnerability and asset owners must confirm exposure scope across Apple, SonicWall GMS, and musl-based Linux deployments. Third-party risk owners must evaluate dependencies on unpatchable vendor platforms. [1]; [2]; [3]

  2. A DigiCert server migration breach exposed customer identity documents, service desk social engineering campaigns bypass multi-factor authentication, and exposure management platforms lack ownership and reachability data for critical findings. These events reveal gaps in supplier data handling, identity verification resilience, and vulnerability governance workflows. Data and privacy owners should assess DigiCert exposure and notification obligations. Identity and access owners must harden service desk verification against impersonation. Risk and control owners should ensure exposure management processes capture accountable system owners and environmental context. [6]; [7]; [9]
