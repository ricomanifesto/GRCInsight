Key messages

  1. Critical vulnerabilities in widely deployed infrastructure demand urgent remediation verification. CISA added N-able MailAssure to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog citing active exploitation, while Broadcom released patches for 23 VMware flaws including a critical authentication bypass in vCenter. Control owners must confirm affected asset inventories and patch deployment status across virtualized and email security platforms. [1]; [3]

  2. Identity-targeted campaigns and supplier breaches elevate access management and third-party risk. The Inception extortion group targets single sign-on credentials through vishing, Radware exposed customer cloud keys via an unsecured management API, and the EU Academy breach compromised personal data. Meanwhile, Iran-aligned Volant Manticore uses IPFS-based backdoors against telecommunications and critical infrastructure. Owners should validate privileged access controls, supplier assurance evidence, data protection monitoring, and detection coverage for novel command-and-control channels. [4]; [6]; [9]; [7]

single sign-on (SSO) credentials exposes credential theft risk

The Inception group's vishing campaign directly targets SSO credentials, challenging authentication assurance for cloud and federated access. Identity owners should review MFA coverage, session revocation procedures, and user awareness training effectiveness.

04

High priorityThird-party risk

unsecured internal Cloud WAF (Web Application Firewall) management API exposes cloud access keys

Radware's exposed management API compromised customer cloud access keys, demonstrating a critical supplier control failure. Supplier risk owners must assess Radware's access scope, request assurance of remediation, and review their own cloud key rotation policies.

Accountable owner
Supplier risk owner
Evidence to request

Decision trigger
If the supplier dependency exists and assurance is insufficient, request evidence or escalate the assurance gap to its owner.
Reference controls
ISO/IEC 27001:2022 A.5.19 (supplier relationships), A.5.21 (ICT supply chain), A.5.22 (monitoring and review of supplier services); NIST CSF 2.0 GV.SC (cybersecurity supply chain risk management)
