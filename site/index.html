Key messages

  1. Critical infrastructure vulnerabilities are under active exploitation across multiple vendors, with CISA adding four flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog citing evidence of active exploitation, a China-nexus group exploiting a VMware vCenter zero-day authentication bypass, and threat actors breaching organizations via a Cisco Expressway flaw shortly after proof-of-concept release. These developments indicate that internet-facing virtualization, collaboration, and application platforms are being targeted at scale, requiring immediate vulnerability management action to identify affected assets, verify patch deployment, and review authentication bypass detection capabilities. [2]; [6]; [5]

  2. A confirmed breach at Bell Canada by an agentic threat actor stole enterprise customer data from provisioning systems, while a ransomware recovery service was indicted for fraudulently reselling attacker decryption keys, and a Linux KVM hypervisor flaw enables cross-tenant memory access on shared AMD hosts. These events reveal evolving threat actor tactics that bypass traditional perimeter controls, supply chain risks in incident response services, and cloud isolation failures that directly threaten data protection assurances. Control owners should confirm data exposure scope, third-party recovery vendor legitimacy, and cloud workload placement on affected hypervisor configurations. [12]; [10]; [4]
